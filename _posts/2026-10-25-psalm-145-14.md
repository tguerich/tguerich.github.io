## A Personal Note

Two Saturdays ago I got sick after doing yard work. I was hungry and dehydrated, and my stomach hurt so much that I had to stop and bend over just to feel better. Janet was out of town for a week, and there were many shelter cats to care for. I lost days of housework and yard work, and I felt, in a word, beset.

A can of tuna and some Gatorade straightened me up. Then this psalm came up in my reading.

## The Text

**Psalm 145:14 (World English Bible)**

“Yahweh upholds all who fall, and raises up all those who are bowed down.”

**Hebrew (Miqra according to the Masorah)**

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size:1.3em">סוֹמֵ֣ךְ יְ֭הֹוָה לְכׇל־הַנֹּפְלִ֑ים וְ֝זוֹקֵ֗ף לְכׇל־הַכְּפוּפִֽים׃</p>

“Yahweh upholds all who fall, and raises up all those who are bowed down.”

Note: Psalm 145 is an alphabetic acrostic; each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. Verse 14 is the samekh (ס) line — fittingly, it opens with סוֹמֵךְ, “upholds.”

## Rare Hebrew Words

**וְזוֹקֵף — wəzôqēp, “and raises up”**

From the root זקף, “to raise upright.” In the Hebrew Bible it occurs only here and in Psalm 146:8. It is common in Aramaic; in Ezra 6:11 it describes a beam set upright.

**הַכְּפוּפִים — hakkəpûpîm, “those bowed down”**

From the root כפף, “to bend.” The participle occurs only here and in Psalm 146:8; the verb also appears at Isaiah 58:5 and Micah 6:6.

Put together, the picture is of people bent under a load, and a God who stands them back up.

## The Septuagint

**Psalm 144:14 LXX (Rahlfs 1935)**

ὑποστηρίζει κύριος πάντας τοὺς καταπίπτοντας καὶ ἀνορθοῖ πάντας τοὺς κατερραγμένους.

“The Lord props up all who are falling, and sets upright again all who are broken down.”

**Word notes**
- ὑποστηρίζει — “props up, supports from underneath” (στήριγμα, a prop; ὑπό, under). It renders the Hebrew סוֹמֵךְ. God gets underneath the one who is going down.
- τοὺς καταπίπτοντας — a present participle: those in the act of falling.
- ἀνορθοῖ — “sets upright again” (from ὀρθός, “straight,” as in orthopedic). It renders the rare זוֹקֵף.
- τοὺς κατερραγμένους — “dashed down, broken down,” a stronger image than the Hebrew “bent over.”

The same pair appears in Psalm 146:8 (LXX 145:8):

κύριος ἀνορθοῖ κατερραγμένους

“The Lord sets upright those who are broken down.”

## ἀνορθόω in the New Testament

The verb ἀνορθόω, “to set upright again,” appears three times in the New Testament.

### Luke 13:10–17 — The Woman Bent Double

A woman had been bent double for eighteen years. Jesus laid his hands on her, and “immediately she was made straight” (ἀνωρθώθη, 13:13). Luke the physician uses the psalm’s own word. This is Psalm 145:14 enacted in person.

### Acts 15:16 — The Fallen Tent of David

**Context.** The Jerusalem Council (about AD 49). Men from Judea were teaching that Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the law of Moses to be saved, so Paul and Barnabas went up to Jerusalem.
- Peter (vv. 7–11): God gave the Holy Spirit to Cornelius’s household, “cleansing their hearts by faith.” “We believe that we are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus, just as they are.”
- Paul and Barnabas (v. 12): They reported the signs and wonders God had done among the Gentiles.
- James (vv. 13–21): He quoted Amos 9:11–12 from the Greek:

“After these things I will return. I will again build the tabernacle of David, which has fallen. I will again build its ruins. I will set it up (ἀνορθώσω), that the rest of men may seek after the Lord, all the Gentiles who are called by my name.”

The Hebrew of Amos has Israel “possess the remnant of Edom”; the Septuagint reads “the rest of mankind will seek the Lord.” James’s argument rests on the Greek.

**Verdict (vv. 19–21, 28–29).** No burden of circumcision was laid on the Gentiles; they were asked to abstain from food offered to idols, from blood, from what is strangled, and from sexual immorality. When the letter was read in Antioch, the church “rejoiced over the encouragement” (v. 31).

### Hebrews 12:12–13 — Straighten the Weak Knees

Διὸ τὰς παρειμένας χεῖρας καὶ τὰ παραλελυμένα γόνατα ἀνορθώσατε, καὶ τροχιὰς ὀρθὰς ποιεῖτε τοῖς ποσὶν ὑμῶν.

“Therefore lift up the hands that hang down and the feeble knees, and make straight paths for your feet, so what is lame may not be dislocated, but rather be healed.”

**Context.** This comes after the great cloud of witnesses (12:1), “looking to Jesus … who endured the cross” (12:2), and the teaching that hardship is the Father’s discipline, yielding “the peaceful fruit of righteousness” (12:5–11). It is not a scolding. It says: because the Father is training you, and Jesus has gone before you, stand up straight again.

## The Isaiah Background

**Isaiah 35:3 (LXX)**

ἰσχύσατε, χεῖρες ἀνειμέναι καὶ γόνατα παραλελυμένα

“Be strong, you slack hands and weak knees.”

Then 35:4: “Say to those who have a fearful heart, ‘Be strong! Don’t be afraid! Behold, your God … will come and save you.’” And in 35:5–6 the blind see and the lame leap like a deer — the desert blooms as God comes to rescue. Jesus points to this passage in Matthew 11:5.

**Key observation.** Hebrews keeps Isaiah’s drooping hands and paralyzed knees (παραλελυμένα, related to our word paralytic), but swaps Isaiah’s verb ἰσχύσατε (“be strong”) for ἀνορθώσατε — the verb of Psalm 145:14 — blending the two passages. The next phrase, “make straight paths” (τροχιὰς ὀρθάς), echoes Proverbs 4:26 LXX, from the same ὀρθός root.

## Fulfillment in Christ

I believe the real fulfillment of this verse is in Christ — in his resurrection, when he was raised from the dust of death by the hand of God. And the Tent of David is really the church universal, the body of believers, stood up by the hand of God.

## The Thread

God sets the fallen upright (Psalm 145). He comes in person to do it (Isaiah 35; Luke 13). He raised Christ and his fallen house, the church (Acts 15). And his people are called to straighten one another’s weak knees along the way (Hebrews 12) — as Keith Lindaman did for me in a dorm Bible study at Rolla in 1970, and as sharing my story with my old friend Larry Stoddard did for me this year.
