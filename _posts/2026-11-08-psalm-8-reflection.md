By Tony Guerich
{: .study-byline}

There is a choir anthem I always hear when I come to Psalm 8: “The Majesty and Glory of Your Name.” I feel that piece in my body, mind, and soul. Performing it is a very moving experience, and my heart starts pounding just thinking about it.

*From the lips of babes and infants you have established strength,*  
*because of your adversaries, that you might silence the enemy and the avenger.*  
— Psalm 8:2, World English Bible

Yet this is the verse that stops me. Right after the psalm sets God’s glory above the heavens, it turns to the smallest voices there are: babes and infants, little ones still nursing. That is where God establishes strength.

The anthem knows this. The choir sings Linda Lee Johnson’s words: “Little children praise Him perfectly / And so would we / And so would we.” A full adult choir, at full voice, points to the children and asks to join them.

A grand anthem could make the opposite seem true. When a choir swells, it is easy to feel the strength is in the sound: the size of the choir, the skill of the singers, the force of the moment. Psalm 8 says otherwise. God can build strength from lips that can barely form words. The choir is not there to outshine the children, but to stand with them.

The psalm does not explain how. It does not claim that a child’s cry, or a choir’s song, makes evil disappear. It says the strength is God’s, set against “the enemy and the avenger” through the least imposing means. The silencing is his work, not our volume.

So when I stand with a choir and feel overwhelmed, I want to remember that our voices are not the foundation. “And so would we” is the longing of grown voices to join the smallest ones.

*Lord, thank you that your strength does not depend on ours. Take our voices, strong or faltering, and let us praise you like your little ones. Silence what opposes you, in your way and in your time. Amen.*
