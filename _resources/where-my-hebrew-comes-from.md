By Tony Guerich
{: .study-byline}

I have been sitting with the first word of Psalm 12. In the Berean Standard Bible it reads, “Help, O LORD, for the godly are no more” (Psalm 12:1, BSB). Help is a good English word. It is also a thin one. Under it stands the Hebrew <bdi lang="he">הוֹשִׁ֣יעָה</bdi> — a bigger word, the same cry that becomes Hosanna, and the root of Jesus’s own name.

Bill Barrick, one of the teachers who helped me learn to see that, quotes a line I have come to love: “Reading the Bible in translation is like kissing your bride through a veil.” I love that picture. I also wanted to get a little closer.

I am a retired layman, not a seminary professor. I have been picking up Biblical Hebrew one teacher at a time, mostly from people who were generous enough to put their classrooms within reach. If you have ever felt that veil and wondered whether this is only for the professionals, here is where my Hebrew comes from.

## Andrew Bartelt, Concordia Seminary, St. Louis

A good deal of my Hebrew comes from [his video series](https://scholar.csl.edu/elementary_hebrew_2015/). Dr. Bartelt teaches the way a patient classroom teacher teaches: letter by letter, then word by word, until a verse begins to open. Concordia Seminary has made the course freely available.

## Bill Barrick, The Master’s Seminary

Bill Barrick has three or more video series, and they are very complete. He is also the teacher who gave me that line about the veil. His lectures were filmed in the classroom at The Master’s Seminary, and he has put them, with his notes, [on his site](https://drbarrick.org/courses/) for anyone to use.

## William Rainey Harper, *Elements of Hebrew by an Inductive Method*

Harper does not ask you to master a grammar book before you meet a verse. He starts in Genesis 1:1 —

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size:1.4em">בְּרֵאשִׁ֖ית בָּרָ֣א אֱלֹהִ֑ים אֵ֥ת הַשָּׁמַ֖יִם וְאֵ֥ת הָאָֽרֶץ׃</p>

“In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth.” (Genesis 1:1, BSB)

— and then teaches you what you need to know to read that verse. Then the next one. It is an old book, now in the public domain, and the method still feels like a kindness: begin with the text, and let the language come as you need it. You can read it at the [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/10030001/).

## Daily Dose of Hebrew

This is a very complete Hebrew course, with daily verse-by-verse parsing videos. You can follow it on the [website](https://dailydoseofscripture.com/languages/hebrew/) or on [X](https://x.com/doseofhebrew). A short video, one verse at a time, is a pace a layman can keep.

Harper’s inductive idea — start with the verse, learn what you need — is even more possible now, because these teachers have brought the classroom onto the web. I am still a student. But the veil is not as heavy as it once was, and you do not have to go to seminary to begin lifting it. Open Genesis 1:1, or the first word of a psalm, and start.
