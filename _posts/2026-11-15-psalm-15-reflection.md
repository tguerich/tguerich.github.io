Psalm 15 opens with a question that will not let a man hide:

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>O LORD, who may abide in Your tent?</em><br />
<em>Who may dwell on Your holy mountain?</em><br />
<em>Psalm 15:1 (BSB)</em></p>

It is a door question. Who may come in? The psalm answers with a portrait: the one who walks with integrity, who speaks the truth from his heart, who does no harm to his neighbor. And then this:

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>who despises the vile but honors those who fear the LORD,</em><br />
<em>who does not revise a costly oath,</em><br />
<em>Psalm 15:4 (BSB)</em></p>

That last line is about a word given and kept, even when it costs. The Hebrew verb there is the same one Psalm 106 uses of Israel at the calf:

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>They exchanged their Glory for the image of a grass-eating ox.</em><br />
<em>Psalm 106:20 (BSB)</em></p>

The man of Psalm 15 will not exchange even a costly oath. Israel exchanged the glory of God itself. Paul takes up that same line in Romans: they "exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images of mortal man" (Romans 1:23, BSB).<br />
I have read that portrait more than once. I know what the psalm is asking. And I know what I have to say.

<p style="text-align:center">And the answer to who is...no one.<br />
Not me.<br />
Revising a costly oath —<br />
How about breaking a costly oath<br />
After many years of marriage.<br />
I was divorced from "the wife of my youth."<br />
No one is the answer.<br />
That's why...</p>

I will not say more than that. The psalm asked who may dwell on the holy mountain, and the true answer is the one I have already given. The psalm just before it has already told the truth. "All have turned away, they have together become corrupt; there is no one who does good, not even one" (Psalm 14:3, BSB).<br />
Paul heard both psalms. In Romans 3 he lets Psalm 14 say it again: "There is no one righteous, not even one. There is no one who understands, no one who seeks God. All have turned away, they have together become worthless; there is no one who does good, not even one" (Romans 3:10–12, BSB). Then comes the sentence that takes us all in: "for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and are justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus" (Romans 3:23–24, BSB).<br />
That's why.<br />
There was one Man who never revised His word, never broke it, never exchanged it. He kept every oath, even when it cost Him the cross. The question comes again in Psalm 24: "Who may ascend the hill of the LORD? Who may stand in His holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart, who does not lift up his soul to an idol or swear deceitfully" (Psalm 24:3–4, BSB). He walked up the holy hill blameless. And He did not go up alone.

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>Therefore He is able to save completely those who draw near to God through Him, since He always lives to intercede for them.</em><br />
<em>Hebrews 7:25 (BSB)</em></p>

I cannot stand in that place on my own record. No one can. That is not the last word. The One who kept His word has gone in, and He brings with Him those who draw near to God through Him. That's why.
