## The verse

> You shall break them with a rod of iron. You shall dash them in pieces like a potter’s vessel.
>
> — Psalm 2:9, World English Bible

Psalm 2 is about God's chosen King. The nations rage against him, and God answers by giving that King the nations as his inheritance. Verse 9 tells us what the King will do with them.

## Same letters, two words

Ancient Hebrew was written without vowels. The first word of this verse is just four letters, and depending on which vowels you add, it can mean "you will break them" or "you will shepherd them." Most English Bibles follow the vowels added centuries later and say "break."

But the oldest readers heard "shepherd." The Jewish scholars who translated the Psalms into Greek before Jesus was born wrote "you will shepherd them." Jerome, translating straight from the Hebrew into Latin around AD 400, wrote the same. And when the book of Revelation quotes this verse three times, its Greek uses the shepherd's word every time (our English Bibles usually render it "rule").

## The shepherd's rod

The rod in Psalm 2 is the same word David uses in Psalm 23: "Your rod and your staff, they comfort me." A shepherd's rod does two things. It protects and guides the sheep, and it breaks the wolf that comes to scatter them. Make it iron, and nothing can break it, but it can break anything that threatens the flock.

So the two readings are not really at odds. The second line of the verse still says the rebels are shattered like clay pots. The shepherd breaks what attacks his flock precisely because he is shepherding it.

## Jesus, the Shepherd-King

Jesus is the Son of Psalm 2 and the Good Shepherd of John 10. Revelation shows both sides of him. In one place he comes with an iron rod against every power set against God. In another, the same verb describes the Lamb's tenderness:

> for the Lamb who is in the middle of the throne shepherds them, and leads them to springs of life-giving waters. And God will wipe away every tear from their eyes.
>
> — Revelation 7:17, World English Bible

## For us today

I find great comfort here. The strength of Christ is not something his people need to fear; it is the very thing that keeps us safe. The rod that no enemy can break is held by the One who laid down his life for the sheep. Whatever is threatening you, sin, fear, grief, or the noise of a raging world, it is the thing that is fragile, not you.

And shepherding is gathering. A shepherd does not only drive off wolves; he goes after the scattered and brings them home. Jesus said, "I have other sheep, which are not of this fold. I must bring them also… They will become one flock with one shepherd." His iron rod clears the way so the flock can be gathered.

1. Where do I need to trust the Shepherd's strength instead of fearing it?
2. Is there someone scattered whom he might be gathering through me?

Psalm 2 ends with an open door: "Blessed are all those who take refuge in him." The King with the iron rod is a shepherd. Come under his care.
