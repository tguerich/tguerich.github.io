By Tony Guerich
{: .study-byline}

We are coming apart today. I see it in declining church membership, in a crisis of confidence in respected institutions, in populations on the move, and in the loss of cultural consensus. This is not only in the United States. It is worldwide. It is rocking long staid places like England, France, and even Japan.

It is a disorienting time. Things we thought would hold are not holding as they once did. I find myself asking what the people of God are supposed to do when the ground itself seems to give way.

Psalm 11 has already asked that question:

*If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do?*  
*Yahweh is in his holy temple. Yahweh is on his throne in heaven. His eyes observe. His eyes examine the children of men.*  
Psalm 11:3–4 (WEB)

The Hebrew of verse 3 puts it just as sharply:

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size:1.4em">כִּ֣י הַ֭שָּׁתוֹת יֵהָרֵס֑וּן צַ֝דִּ֗יק מַה־פָּעָֽל׃</p>

“If the foundations are destroyed, what can the righteous do?”

It is a real question. David does not pretend the foundations are fine. He names the danger, and then he does something surprising. He does not answer with a plan. He answers with a location:

*Yahweh is in his holy temple. Yahweh is on his throne in heaven. His eyes observe. His eyes examine the children of men.*

The foundations can crack—the church roll, the institutions we once trusted, the old consensus in places that seemed settled for generations. The temple does not. God has not stepped off his throne. His eyes are still open.

What strikes me is that Jesus used that same word, temple, of himself. In the ancient Greek translation of the Psalms, the Septuagint, the verse is numbered 10:4, and it begins κύριος ἐν ναῷ ἁγίῳ αὐτοῦ. The word ναός is the word for temple. Jesus says, “Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up” (John 2:19, WEB). The Greek of that command is Λύσατε τὸν ναὸν τοῦτον, and the word for temple is again ναός. John tells us plainly what he meant: “But he spoke of the temple of his body” (John 2:21, WEB).

Jesus himself was the temple. He was the tabernacle. God dwelling among men. John’s Gospel says it at the start: “The Word became flesh and lived among us” (John 1:14, WEB). The verb there is ἐσκήνωσεν ἐν ἡμῖν: he tabernacled, he pitched his tent among us.

So the temple men could tear down was the one he raised. When the foundations shake, we are not left staring at the rubble, as if the only answer were a better plan or a sturdier institution. The answer is still a place, and a Person. God is in his temple. The foundations may give way. He does not.

*Lord, when the foundations shake, and when we come apart, fix our eyes where David fixed his. You are in your holy temple. You are on your throne in heaven. Your eyes observe; your eyes examine the children of men. Thank you that you did not remain far off, but tabernacled among us, and that the temple they destroyed is the one you raised. Keep us trusting you, and not the ground under our feet. Amen.*
