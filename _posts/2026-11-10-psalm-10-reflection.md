<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size:1.4em">לָמָ֣ה יְ֭הֹוָה תַּעֲמֹ֣ד בְּרָח֑וֹק<br>תַּ֝עְלִ֗ים לְעִתּ֥וֹת בַּצָּרָֽה׃</p>

That is how Psalm 10 begins in Hebrew, and it is where I want to begin. The Name comes right after “why,” before the verb <bdi lang="he">תַּעֲמֹד</bdi> (“you stand”) even arrives. It is a vocative, a direct cry: “Why, O LORD…” The psalmist calls on God by name first, and only then asks why he stands far off.

The World English Bible, like several other English translations, moves the Name to the end:

*Why do you stand far off, Yahweh?*  
*Why do you hide yourself in times of trouble?*  
Psalm 10:1 (WEB)

The King James Version, the New King James, the NASB, and the JPS Tanakh of 1917 do the same. They follow ordinary English order, and something is lost. In Hebrew the appeal comes first, and the complaint after.

A few translations keep the Hebrew order. The Berean Standard Bible reads, “Why, O LORD, do You stand far off?” Young’s Literal has, “Why, Jehovah, dost Thou stand at a distance?” Rotherham’s Emphasized Bible comes closest in feel: “Wherefore, O Yahweh, shouldst thou stand afar off?”

I found myself asking:

Why did we put You so far off, oh Yahweh, in our translation when you were right up front in Hebrew? Selah

That question will not quite leave me alone. Is Yahweh really hiding or is it we who are looking somewhere else?

Look at verse 4:

<p dir="rtl" lang="he" style="font-size:1.4em">רָשָׁ֗ע כְּגֹ֣בַהּ אַ֭פּוֹ בַּל־יִדְרֹ֑שׁ אֵ֥ין אֱ֝לֹהִ֗ים כׇּל־מְזִמּוֹתָֽיו׃</p>

*The wicked, in the pride of his face,*  
*has no room in his thoughts for God.*  
Psalm 10:4 (WEB)

Taken word for word, the Hebrew says: “The wicked, by the height of his nose, does not seek; ‘There is no God’ is all his scheming.” <bdi lang="he">אַפּוֹ</bdi> means “his nose.” Here his nose is up in the air. While he is looking down his nose, he cannot look up. <bdi lang="he">בַּל־יִדְרֹשׁ</bdi> means “he does not seek.” The verb is <bdi lang="he">דָּרַשׁ</bdi>, the ordinary word for seeking or inquiring of God. And <bdi lang="he">אֵין אֱלֹהִים</bdi>, “there is no God,” is the same phrase the fool says in his heart in Psalm 14:1.

So the hiddenness in verse 1 and the not-seeking in verse 4 sit only three verses apart. The psalmist is describing the wicked man. But the honest application is “we.” Our noses go up too.

The psalm does not leave us there. God is not as far off as we feared.

*But you do see trouble and grief.*  
*You consider it to take it into your hand.*  
*You help the victim and the fatherless.*  
Psalm 10:14 (WEB)

And he hears the humble:

*Yahweh, you have heard the desire of the humble.*  
*You will prepare their heart.*  
Psalm 10:17 (WEB)

*Lord, when we think you are far off, turn our faces back toward you. Keep us from looking down our noses. Teach us to seek you, and to trust that you see, and that you hear the desire of the humble. Amen.*
