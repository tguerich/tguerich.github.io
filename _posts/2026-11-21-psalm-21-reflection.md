A Reflection on Psalm 21
{: .study-subtitle}

Tony Guerich · November 21, 2026
{: .study-byline}

When we were kids, we lived in an area where there were remnants of farms and fields, still untouched by subdivisions.

There was no trash service.

So my brother Mark and I would burn the trash that was burnable in a trash barrel out behind the house.

There was a grate on the top, but you still had to watch it.

On a windy day, part of the Post-Dispatch Sunday supplement might blow out and land in the grass and start a little fire, and you had to go stamp it out with your feet.

That is how ordinary fire works. It has to eat. It needs fuel, and it consumes it.

One day, when I was a little older, we started a trash fire.

I looked at it and thought, I have to go to church. I hope this fire’s gonna be okay.

The wind wasn’t real strong, and usually at that strength nothing would ever blow out of the barrel.

So I got in the car and drove to the Catholic church in town.

Right about the time I was going in, the wind started to pick up, and I figured, by this time that trash is burned out.

About halfway through the service, the siren went off at the volunteer fire department, right next door to the church.

Several of the men in the church were volunteer firemen, including the chief, who lived right across the street from both the church and the firehouse.

All those men ran out, and I remember thinking, I hope our house isn’t burning down from that fire.

I thought, nah.

So I sat through the rest of the Mass, and then I drove home.

Right as I was coming around a bend in the road, going up the hill, I could see all the fire trucks at the top, and all the men out with their hose whips beating down the brush fire on our property.

I thought, oh my goodness, I hope our house didn’t burn down.

I went up to the top of the hill.

The square where our house was, we kept mowed with a riding lawnmower.

All that grass was nice and green, and the fire didn’t come up there.

But the rest of the property looked like World War Three.

Smoke going up, all black.

“The smoke rising from the land like smoke from a furnace” (Genesis 19:28, BSB).

The fire had stopped at the edge of the green, mowed square.

Everything else had burned.

What kind of a man am I?

Am I still young and green, where the flame won’t consume me?

Or am I old and dry?

Psalm 21 takes that ordinary fire and makes it a picture of judgment.

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>You will place them in a fiery furnace</em><br />
<em>at the time of Your appearing.</em><br />
<em>In His wrath the LORD will engulf them,</em><br />
<em>and the fire will consume them.</em><br />
Psalm 21:9 (BSB)</p>

The Hebrew is plainer than “fiery furnace.”

It says “like an oven of fire.”<sup id="fnref1"><a href="#fn1">1</a></sup>

An oven was a household thing, as common as a barrel in the yard.

Fire opens the picture, and the same word for fire closes the verse.

And that fire “will consume them.” The word is the ordinary word for eating.

So I went looking for that fire in other places, and I landed at the bush.

<p style="margin-left:36pt"><em>There the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a blazing fire from within a bush. Moses saw the bush ablaze with fire, but it was not consumed.</em><br />
Exodus 3:2 (BSB)</p>

What was remarkable?

A fireproof bush?

No.

A fire that needed no fuel.

Ordinary fire lives on what it burns. This fire took nothing from the bush. It burned on its own.

A few verses later, Moses hears the Name that explains it: “I AM WHO I AM” (Exodus 3:14, BSB).

The fire needs nothing, because He needs nothing.

At the bush, the fire does not consume.

In Deuteronomy, “the LORD your God is a consuming fire” (Deuteronomy 4:24, BSB). The fire is what He is.

In Psalm 21, the fire consumes the King’s enemies.

Daniel saw the other side of the same contrast.

The furnace was built to destroy three men who belonged to God. It did not.

“The fire had no effect on the bodies of these men... there was no smell of fire on them” (Daniel 3:27, BSB).

And a fourth man was walking in the fire with them (Daniel 3:25).

His presence made the difference, just as “Your appearing” does in the psalm.

At Pentecost, “they saw tongues like flames of fire that separated and came to rest on each of them” (Acts 2:3, BSB).

The bush again. Fire resting on people, and not consuming them.

What the fire does depends on what it meets, not on what it needs.

The green, mowed square came through. The dry brush burned.

“It is a fearful thing to fall into the hands of the living God” (Hebrews 10:31, BSB).

“For our God is a consuming fire” (Hebrews 12:29, BSB).<sup id="fnref2"><a href="#fn2">2</a></sup>

## Notes

<sup id="fn1"><a href="#fnref1">1</a></sup> The Hebrew text is from the Miqra according to the Masorah on Sefaria. English quotations are from the Berean Standard Bible, via bible.helloao.org. In the Hebrew numbering, the title is verse 1, so this line stands in verse 10; in English it is verse 9. The phrase is <span lang="he" dir="rtl">כְּתַנּוּר אֵשׁ</span>, “like an oven of fire.” The verse ends with the same word, <span lang="he" dir="rtl">אֵשׁ</span>, “fire.” The verb “consume” (<span lang="he" dir="rtl">וְתֹאכְלֵם</span>, “and it will eat them”) shares its root with “it was not consumed” in Exodus 3:2 (<span lang="he" dir="rtl">אֻכָּל</span>) and “a consuming fire” in Deuteronomy 4:24 (<span lang="he" dir="rtl">אֵשׁ אֹכְלָה</span>).

<sup id="fn2"><a href="#fnref2">2</a></sup> Hebrews 12:29 quotes Deuteronomy 4:24 from the Greek Old Testament (Rahlfs, 1935): <span lang="grc">πῦρ καταναλίσκον</span>, “a consuming fire,” with “our God” (SBLGNT) in place of “the LORD your God.”
